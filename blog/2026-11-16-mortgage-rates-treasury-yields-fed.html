Mortgage Rates Fall to 7.11% for a Fourth Week While the 10-Year Treasury Yield Climbs to 5.15%

Underlytix Desk4 min read

Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the 30-year fixed-rate average at 7.11% for the week of November 12, 2026, down from 7.18% on November 5 and 7.24% on October 29. It was the fourth consecutive weekly decline and the lowest reading in seven weeks. The 15-year average eased to 6.45% from 6.57%. Relief is relative, however: a year earlier the 30-year average was 6.24% and the 15-year was 5.49%, so borrowers are paying 87 and 96 basis points more than they were at this point last year.

The bond market moved the other way during the same stretch. According to the U.S. Treasury's daily par yield curve, the 10-year yield rose from 4.84% on November 5 to 5.15% on November 13, and the 2-year yield rose from 4.46% to 4.88%. Because the Freddie Mac survey covers a Thursday-through-Wednesday window, it captures rates before much of that climb. Comparing the 7.11% survey average with the 5.10% 10-year yield on November 12 implies a spread of roughly two percentage points, a calculation that is approximate given the timing difference between the two series.

Sources: Freddie Mac PMMS, as of November 12, 2026; U.S. Treasury daily par yield curve, through November 13, 2026 (no November 11 row published).

The policy backdrop adds uncertainty. At its October 28 meeting the Federal Open Market Committee held the federal funds target range at 3.75% to 4.00% by a 10-2 vote. Governors Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissented, and both preferred a quarter-point increase. The statement says inflation remains elevated. The next meeting is scheduled for December 8-9 and includes a Summary of Economic Projections. Rising Treasury yields combined with two dissents in favor of a hike suggest the recent easing in mortgage rates may not be durable, although that is an interpretation rather than a forecast.

For investors and lenders, the practical issue is that debt service assumptions made at a 7.11% benchmark can be stale within days. Variable pricing, rate lock timing, and the gap between conventional benchmarks and investor loan pricing all affect whether a property still clears its coverage and leverage thresholds. Deal files built on last week's rate sheet deserve a fresh look before commitments are made.

What this means for deals under review this month: Underlytix's Capital Readiness assessment tests a deal against DSCR, LTV, and cash-to-close in about 60 seconds. When benchmark rates move week to week, rerunning the assessment at the current rate shows whether debt service coverage still holds, how much additional cash a higher payment or lower proceeds could require, and which lenders remain a fit. The data shows a market in transition: mortgage rates have retreated for four weeks, but Treasury yields and a divided Fed point to a path that is far from settled. Investors who revisit their numbers each week, rather than anchoring to a single survey reading, will be better positioned when the December meeting arrives.
