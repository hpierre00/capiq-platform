DSCR and Bridge Rates Diverge: A 6.375% Floor Versus 9% to 12%

Underlytix Desk4 min read

Investors comparing financing for deals this week are looking at a wide spread between loan types. A DSCR rate sheet published on September 30, 2026 by DSCR Direct showed 30-year DSCR pricing starting at 6.375%, with posted scenarios running as high as 8.875%. The low end applied to a purchase with a 760 FICO score, 60% LTV, and a 1.25 DSCR. A comparable purchase at 640 FICO, 75% LTV, and a 1.00 DSCR priced at 8.375%. The same survey lists a 6.750% quote for a 780 FICO, 75% LTV, 1.25 DSCR purchase.

Conventional pricing sits in a similar neighborhood. Freddie Mac reported that the 30-year fixed-rate mortgage averaged 7.03% for the week ending September 24, 2026, up from 6.95% the prior week and 6.30% a year earlier. The 15-year averaged 6.42%. These surveys use different methods and dates, so a DSCR quote below the Freddie Mac average should not be read as a like-for-like comparison; owner-occupied conventional loans and investor DSCR loans are priced on different grids.

Sources: DSCR Direct (September 30, 2026), Freddie Mac PMMS (week ending September 24, 2026), HardMoneyScout (October 1, 2026), retrieved October 1, 2026.

Short-term capital is a different story. HardMoneyScout, updated October 1, 2026, lists bridge loan rates of 9% to 12% at typical LTVs of 65% to 80%, with 1 to 2 origination points. Its network average across hard money products is 10.35%, and the lowest rate in its network is 8.9%. For an investor weighing a bridge-to-DSCR-refinance strategy, the gap between roughly 6.4% and 9% or more is the carrying cost of the transition, before points.

The macro backdrop adds a date to watch. A lender rate commentary dated September 17, 2026 from Ridge Street Capital reports that the Federal Reserve raised its target range to 3.75% to 4.00% on September 16, 2026. The next FOMC meeting runs October 27 to 28, with the decision due October 28 at 2:00 PM ET. Lenders who price off Treasury yields may adjust rate sheets around that date, so assumptions made today about a refinance takeout can move before a deal closes.

What this means for deals under review this month: A DSCR loan is underwritten on the property's income, so the same asset can price very differently depending on its coverage ratio, leverage, and the borrower's cash position. For the weeks ahead, the practical discipline is to test each deal against more than one rate scenario. A deal that clears a 1.25 DSCR at the low end of the range may fall short at the high end, and a bridge position that pencils at 9% may not at 12%. Reviewing coverage, leverage, and liquidity early keeps those conversations grounded in numbers rather than assumptions.
