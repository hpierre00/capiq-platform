Single-Family Rental Demand Jumps 21.6% as a Typical Buyer Payment Runs $771 Above Rent

Underlytix Desk4 min read

Rental demand is accelerating at the same moment ownership is getting harder to finance. Zillow's rental market report, released October 15, 2026 and covering September, found renter demand up 19.2% from a year earlier. Demand for single-family rentals rose 21.6%, roughly double the 10.1% increase for multifamily units. Investors who hold or underwrite single-family rentals are seeing the strongest tenant pull in the property type.

The affordability gap explains the shift. Zillow puts the typical single-family rent at $2,266 a month, which is $771 less than the $3,037 monthly payment on a typical home bought with 10% down and a 30-year fixed loan. That buyer payment is up 5.0% in a year. A new renter spends 26.3% of income on housing, essentially unchanged from last year, while a new homeowner spends 41.3%. Freddie Mac's weekly survey on November 12, 2026 put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.11%, down from 7.18% the week before but well above 6.24% a year earlier.

Income requirements reinforce the point. Zillow estimates a household needs $77,278 a year to afford a typical rental but $121,496 to afford a typical home, a difference of $44,218. For landlords, that gap is the structural reason tenants stay in place longer and applicants for single-family rentals keep arriving, even as headline rent growth stays muted. For lenders, it is a reminder that borrower demand for rental-property financing is being driven by affordability pressure, not by a strengthening rental market.

Sources: Zillow rental market report, October 15, 2026 (September 2026 data); cross-checked against CRE Daily.

Rent growth itself remains modest, which matters for underwriting. Apartment List's October 2026 report, published October 28, shows a national median rent of $1,384, up 0.1% year over year. That is the first annual gain in more than three years, but rents fell 0.3% from September, the second straight monthly decline. Its vacancy index stands at 7.0%, down from a February 2026 peak of 7.3%. Zillow's typical rent, which covers a different mix of homes and a different method, rose 2.7% over the same twelve months to $1,932. The two measures agree on direction, not magnitude.

For investors and lenders, the combination cuts two ways. Strong demand for single-family rentals supports occupancy assumptions, and expensive ownership keeps would-be buyers in the rental pool. But thin rent growth means the numerator in a debt service coverage calculation is not rising quickly, while financing costs near 7% hold the denominator high. A deal that pencils at last year's rates may not clear a lender's coverage threshold today, even with a full rent roll.

What this means for deals under review this month: Demand headlines do not change the arithmetic of a single deal. The practical read this week is to underwrite to in-place rents, not to demand growth. Rental demand is strong, but the data shows rents moving sideways while ownership costs stay elevated. Deals that hold their coverage ratios under those conditions are the ones worth taking to lenders.
