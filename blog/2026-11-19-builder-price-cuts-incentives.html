Builders Cut Prices at a 46% Clip and Offer Incentives at 68% as Confidence Falls to 25

Underlytix Desk4 min read

Homebuilders are discounting at the widest rate since the pandemic, and they are doing it while confidence sinks. The NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index fell four points to 25 in November, the lowest reading since April 2012, according to the survey released on November 17, 2026. Any reading below 50 means more builders view conditions as poor than good.

The discounting data is the most useful part of the release for deal underwriting. Forty-six percent of builders reported cutting prices, the highest share in the post-Covid period, and the average reduction held at 6 percent for an eighth straight month. Sixty-eight percent used sales incentives, also a post-Covid high, marking the 20th consecutive month in which at least 60 percent of builders did so. Price cuts and incentives are no longer a reaction to a soft patch; they are the operating norm for new construction.

Demand indicators explain why. Current sales conditions fell six points to 28, six-month sales expectations slipped two points to 30, and prospective buyer traffic fell two points to 16, the lowest in 15 years outside the start of the pandemic in 2020. Regionally, the three-month moving averages show the West weakest at 23, followed by the South at 27, the Northeast at 34 and the Midwest at 35.

Sources: NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index, released November 17, 2026 (via NAHB and Eye on Housing).

Supply and financing complete the picture. The Census Bureau reported that new single-family home sales ran at a 676,000 annual pace in September 2026, with a median sales price of $400,400 and 8.4 months of supply (release dated October 27, 2026). Meanwhile, the Freddie Mac survey put the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.11% on November 12, 2026, against 6.24% a year earlier. Builders are absorbing part of that gap through concessions, which can make a new-construction purchase price a weaker guide to true cost than the contract price suggests.

For investors and lenders, the practical issue is that headline prices and effective prices are diverging. A buyer who receives a rate buydown or closing credit has a different cash-to-close and a different carrying cost than the list price implies, and comparable-sales analysis can lag the discounting. Underwriters should confirm what concessions are embedded in any new-construction comp before relying on it.

What this means for deals under review this month: New-construction discounts change the inputs that matter most to a lender.
