DSCR Rates Hold Near 7% While Conventional Sits at 6.7%

Underlytix Desk4 min read

Rate surveys published this week show DSCR loan pricing running from 6.375 percent to 7.875 percent, with the tightest terms reserved for borrowers with FICO scores above 740 and loan to value ratios at 75 percent or lower on single family rentals, according to HomeAbroad's August 2026 rate data. HomeAbroad's own baseline par rate sits at 6.12 percent for domestic investors, while Griffin Funding lists 30 year fixed DSCR options starting at 6.375 percent and one year ARMs from 5.375 percent. DSCR Direct advertises rates as low as 6.250 percent for its strongest files.

For comparison, the 30 year conventional average landed between 6.54 percent and 6.76 percent this week. NerdWallet reported 6.54 percent APR on August 8, Bankrate and Zillow both showed 6.75 percent, and Freddie Mac's weekly survey came in at 6.69 percent on August 6. That puts the DSCR to conventional spread at roughly 0 to 120 basis points on the low end for the strongest DSCR files, widening past 100 basis points for lower credit or higher leverage deals, consistent with lenders' general guidance that DSCR pricing runs 0.5 to 2 percentage points above conventional.

Private and bridge capital remains the most expensive tier. Market surveys this month put bridge loan rates in a broad 9 to 14 percent range, with private money bridge financing on value add deals typically pricing between 8.5 and 12 percent depending on leverage and borrower experience. The strongest borrowers with the cleanest deals can still find published rates starting near 7.75 percent, but most investors relying on bridge or hard money should expect to underwrite closer to the middle of that range, plus origination points and shorter, six to 24 month terms.

What this means for deals under review this month: The gap between DSCR and conventional pricing rewards documentation and deal structure, not just credit score. Underlytix's Capital Readiness assessment checks DSCR, LTV, and cash to close in about 60 seconds, so investors and lenders can see where a deal sits against current market pricing before it goes to underwriting instead of after a lender's decline.

The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.50 to 3.75 percent at its June meeting and again after its July 28 to 29 meeting, keeping the backdrop for mortgage pricing largely unchanged through the summer. The next FOMC decision lands September 15 to 16, alongside an updated Summary of Economic Projections, and will be the next real catalyst for movement in both conventional and investor loan pricing. Until then, the spread between DSCR, conventional, and private capital documented this week is likely to hold, making rate shopping and capital readiness prep worthwhile before that meeting rather than after it.
