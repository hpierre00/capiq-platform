Existing-Home Sales Fall to a 3.75 Million Pace, the Lowest Since 2010, as Supply Reaches 5.1 Months

Underlytix Desk4 min read

The National Association of Realtors reported on November 12, 2026 that existing-home sales ran at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.75 million in October. That is 5.5% below September and 8.8% below the 4.11 million pace of October 2025, and it is the slowest rate since August 2010. NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun attributed the decline to mortgage rates, which he said had reached their highest levels since 2000 outside one month in 2023.

The rate backdrop is documented. NAR cites a Freddie Mac 30-year fixed average of 7.33% for October, compared with 6.86% in September and 6.25% in October 2025. Freddie Mac's weekly survey dated November 12, 2026 showed 7.11%, down from 7.18% the prior week but still well above the 6.24% recorded a year earlier. Buyers are therefore transacting at borrowing costs roughly one percentage point higher than a year ago.

Supply is the other half of the story. Unsold inventory reached 1.59 million homes, up 1.3% from September and 4.6% from a year earlier. At the current sales pace that equals 5.1 months of supply, up from 4.7 months in September and 4.4 months a year ago, and the highest reading since November 2015 according to Real Estate News. The median existing-home price was $416,700, up 0.4% from $414,900 a year earlier, extending the streak of annual gains to 40 months. Median days on market were 35, compared with 33 in September and 34 a year earlier.

Sources: NAR existing-home sales release (November 12, 2026, October 2026 data); mortgage rates are Freddie Mac monthly averages as cited by NAR.

For investors, one data point deserves attention. NAR reported that individual investors and second-home buyers accounted for 20% of October transactions, unchanged from September and up from 16% a year earlier. Cash sales were 29% of transactions, up from 27% in September, while first-time buyers fell to 29% from 32% a year ago. Fewer owner-occupant buyers and a larger investor share suggest that financed, rate-sensitive demand is the segment pulling back. NAR also noted that distressed sales remain near 2% of transactions, so the slowdown reflects affordability rather than forced selling.

Longer selling times and rising supply shift negotiating leverage toward buyers, but they do not lower the cost of debt. At a 7% to 7.5% note rate, debt service coverage and cash-to-close requirements tighten even when the purchase price is negotiable. A deal that cleared underwriting twelve months ago at a rate near 6.25% may not clear today on the same rent roll.

What this means for deals under review this month: With inventory rising and sellers more open to terms, more properties are worth a second look, but each one has to be tested against current rates. Underlytix's Capital Readiness assessment evaluates DSCR, LTV and cash-to-close in about 60 seconds, so an investor can see which of several listings still pencil at today's financing costs before spending time on offers or lender calls.

Analysts from Realtor.com and Zonda quoted by RISMedia expect buyers to return only modestly as rates ease, and buyers are expected to commit in force only once they believe the relief will last. Until borrowing costs fall materially, the market is likely to favor investors who verify their financing position early and negotiate from a documented basis.
