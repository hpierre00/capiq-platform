Existing-Home Sales Hold at a 3.99 Million Pace as Supply Reaches 4.8 Months

Underlytix Desk4 min read

The National Association of Realtors reported on October 13, 2026 that existing-home sales for September ran at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.99 million, up 1.5% from August but down 2.2% from September 2025. The median existing-home price was $417,500, up 1.3% from $412,300 a year earlier, which marks continued but modest appreciation.

Supply is the more important shift. Inventory reached 1.60 million units, up 1.9% from August and up 4.6% from a year ago, equal to 4.8 months of supply versus 4.5 months in September 2025. Median time on market was 33 days, up from 31 days in August and unchanged from a year earlier. A market near five months of supply is closer to balance than the tight conditions of recent years, which gives buyers more selection and more room to negotiate.

Sources: National Association of Realtors, Existing-Home Sales for September 2026, released October 13, 2026; cross-checked against Inman.

Buyer composition also moved. Investors and second-home buyers accounted for 20% of sales, up from 15% in August and a year earlier. Cash sales fell to 27% of transactions from 30% a year ago, and first-time buyers made up 31% of sales. Financed purchases are therefore a larger share of the market, which places more weight on underwriting quality for every deal.

Financing costs remain a constraint. Freddie Mac reported a 6.86% average 30-year fixed rate for September, up from 6.67% in August and 6.35% a year earlier. Regionally, September sales were flat in the Midwest, rose 2.8% in both the South and West, and fell 2.1% in the Northeast. Median prices rose 4.7% year over year in the Northeast and 3.1% in the Midwest, but only 0.2% in the South and fell 0.3% in the West to $613,600.

What this means for deals under review this month: Rising inventory and a higher financed share make deal-level numbers decisive. For lenders, the data points to a market that is stable in volume but increasingly selective. Longer listing times and 4.8 months of supply suggest that price and terms, rather than speed alone, will separate viable deals from marginal ones this month. These figures are national and September-based, so local conditions and the October data, due later this month, should be checked before committing capital.
